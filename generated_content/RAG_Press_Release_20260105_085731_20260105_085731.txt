## Press Release

### Immediate Release

**California's Hidden Pollution Crisis: A Growing Threat to Public Health**

San Diego, CA - Today, a pressing concern regarding pollution in California's waterways is gaining attention. As the state navigates its complex infrastructure and environmental regulations, a pressing issue has emerged that necessitates immediate action.

The recent release of research on air quality highlights the alarming levels of toxic substances, or "IT," found in our atmosphere. According to experts at San Diego State University, IT was discovered flowing into our environment through concentrated wastewater and industrial waste from manufacturing facilities across the border (Source: 1).

This pollution has severe implications for the health of Californians and the environment. As noted by Dr. Helga Greetings, "Unless we address this industrial chemical pollution going into the river, we're not going to solve this problem long-term" (Source: 3). The long-term effects on our communities, wildlife, and marine life are dire.

### Core Details and Significance

The pollution crisis in California's waterways is a growing concern due to several factors:

*   Concentrated wastewater and industrial waste from manufacturing facilities in Tijuana, Mexico, are contaminating our rivers.
*   The lack of regulation and enforcement on these facilities is allowing this pollution to continue unchecked.
*   Research has shown that IT can be found in the air we breathe, with levels exceeding 4500 parts per billion (ppb) in some areas.

### Quotes from Key Stakeholders

"It's a ticking time bomb. We need to address this issue now" - Dr. Helga Greetings, Coastal Defenders

"We have the authority to hold these facilities accountable, but we need to use it" - Paula, San Diego State University

### Calls to Action

To address the pollution crisis in California's waterways, the following actions are necessary:

*   Implement stricter regulations on manufacturing facilities in Tijuana, Mexico
*   Increase enforcement of current environmental laws and regulations
*   Invest in research and development of new technologies to mitigate the effects of IT
