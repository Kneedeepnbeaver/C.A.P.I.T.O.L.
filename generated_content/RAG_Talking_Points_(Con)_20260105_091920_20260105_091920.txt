## Talking Points (Con)

As we consider [Bill Name], we must carefully evaluate the potential consequences of this legislation. While some may argue that it addresses a critical issue, I firmly believe that we have more significant concerns to address.

### Lead with the most serious concern
#### Environmental Impact

The proposed legislation may inadvertently harm the very environment it aims to protect. By streamlining the approval process for air pollution technologies, we risk overlooking crucial environmental assessments and ignoring potential long-term consequences. According to Dr. Patricia Pfautch from the California Legislature's Senate Bill Policy Committee Analysis (Source 6), the bill "shall not be construed to limit protections provided to employees by specified sections of the Labor Code, the Government Code, or other provisions of California law." This suggests that we must ensure our environmental regulations are robust enough to safeguard public health and safety.

#### Lack of Public Input

Testimony from [Senator Name] (Source 5) highlights the concern about the potential for a "months-long process" without an APA waiver, which could lead to a lack of public input on critical issues. This raises serious questions about the legitimacy of our decision-making processes and the need for more transparent and inclusive procedures.

### Highlight costs or unintended consequences
#### Unintended Consequences

The bill's exemption from the APA process may have unforeseen consequences, such as increased reliance on untested technologies or a lack of accountability in the development of new air pollution controls. According to [Senator Name], "there would still be some public process" even without an APA waiver (Source 7). However, this may not be sufficient to ensure that our decisions are well-informed and align with the public interest.

### Use concrete examples
#### Example: Prop One-Bond

The bill's proponents often cite the success of Proposition One-Bond as a model for efficiency and effectiveness. However, as [Senator Name] noted, "we were able to quickly get things out because without having to go through a full process" (Source 7). While this may have been beneficial in certain contexts, it is essential to recognize that our current infrastructure is not equipped to handle the complexities of air pollution control.
